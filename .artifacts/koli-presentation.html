The case for Koli

A creator-marketing agency run out of the spreadsheet you already know how to use — built for the boutique operators that enterprise influencer platforms were never priced, or built, for.

Runs on Google Sheets + Apps Script Paired with Send to Koli, a Chrome extension Distributed by Make a copy, not install
A1

What Koli is

Koli is a full influencer-marketing operations layer built directly inside a Google Sheet. There's no dashboard to log into and no database to trust with your creator relationships: the Sheet is the product. Every channel you vet, every grade computed, every sponsor mention detected, every outreach draft written lands in cells you can sort, filter, share, and export the moment they're written — because they always were just cells.

A companion Chrome extension, Send to Koli, extends that same Sheet out onto the open web: right-click a YouTube channel anywhere you find one, pull a live grade-and-stats preview, and commit it to your tracked list without ever leaving the tab you were already on.

B1

The problem it's solving

01

Vetting a creator by hand doesn't scale past a handful a week

Checking real engagement, posting cadence, past sponsors, and audience fit means opening a dozen tabs per channel. Most of that work is mechanical — it just hasn't been automated for anyone below enterprise scale.

02

The tools that automate it are priced for teams that don't exist yet

Platforms built for this — CreatorIQ, Grin, Aspire — sell to brand marketing departments with dedicated budget and a procurement process. A one-person talent manager or a three-person boutique agency is not who those contracts are written for.

03

Generic outreach gets deleted, and AI has made it worse, not better

Creators now get more AI-drafted pitches than ever, and they can tell. An email that opens with "I hope this finds you well" and cites a subscriber count is competing with a flood of identical ones — the bar for sounding like you actually watch the channel just went up.

C1

Who it's for

Boutique creator-partnership agencies

Small teams running deal flow for a real roster of creators, without an ops department to build internal tooling.

Solo & independent talent managers

One person handling vetting, outreach, and deal tracking end to end — the exact user an enterprise seat price prices out.

In-house brand partnerships, small teams

A brand sourcing and managing its own creator relationships directly, before it's big enough to justify a CreatorIQ contract.

E1

What actually sets it apart

The hook comes from a real rewatch spike
Outreach emails don't sample a transcript at random — they're built around the exact moment YouTube's own "Most Replayed" heatmap shows a creator's audience actually rewound to watch again. That's evidence something specific landed, not a guess at what might be interesting.
A prompt engineered against sounding like AI
The draft generator runs against an explicit, growing list of banned tells — em dashes as connectors, "I hope this finds you well," "resonate," "elevate," a direct ask for a reply — because a pitch that reads as AI-written is worse than no pitch at all in a 2026 inbox.
Sponsor history, without watching a single video
Brand mentions are pulled automatically from a creator's caption transcripts and cross-referenced against known sponsor segments, with a timestamp as evidence — turning what used to be an afternoon of manual scrubbing into a background pass.
A grade that's declared, not dressed up as standard
Koli's A–F score is stated plainly as its own formula — growth momentum weighted highest, comment authenticity second, audience-tier a smaller bonus — not marketed as an industry benchmark it isn't.
Capture from anywhere, preview before you commit
Send to Koli works from any tab, not just an in-app search box. Pull Stats shows the real grade card first; only "Add to Sheet" actually writes the row — looking costs nothing.
No install, no procurement, no lock-in
Koli is distributed as a Sheet you copy, running on your own Google account. There's no vendor to onboard with and nothing proprietary to migrate out of later — the data was always just yours.
"Enterprise-grade" should be something a user concludes on their own halfway through using it — never something the product has to say about itself.

H1

Beyond 2026

Three real horizons, deliberately kept distinct — near-term product work, a second layer of operational depth, and a much bigger platform bet underneath both.

01

Closing the gaps already visible

Region-aware audience estimates beyond a fixed country list, a real contract template library to match what boutique-tier competitors already ship, and deeper campaign management than a single stage dropdown. Scoped, concrete, next in line.

02

The layer enterprise platforms charge for

Gifting, creator payments, affiliate/UTM attribution, and UGC rights management — each its own subsystem, not a quick add. This is specifically the depth CreatorIQ and Grin's enterprise pricing is buying: building it at Koli's price point is the actual competitive wedge, not a nice-to-have.

03

Koli as a flagship, not the whole company

The bigger bet: an ambient AI layer that acts inside the Sheet instead of a chat box beside it, a plugin marketplace for third-party data sources, and Sheetgo-style automation wired in natively. If it happens, Koli becomes the first vertical built on a general, agent-native spreadsheet platform — not the platform itself. Worth building, but only once it can be resourced as its own effort rather than competing for attention against nearer, smaller wins.
